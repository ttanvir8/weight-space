TASK. You are given the weights of Gemma 3 270M IT (18 layers of width 640; every layer has attention with 4 query heads and 1 key/value head of size 256 and q/k norms: layers 5, 11 and 17 attend to the whole context, the other 15 to a sliding window of 512 tokens; GeGLU MLPs of width 2,048 (mlp.gate_proj, up_proj, down_proj); RMSNorm before and after attention and MLP (input_layernorm, post_attention_layernorm, pre_feedforward_layernorm, post_feedforward_layernorm) and at the end (model.norm); a tied 262,144-token embedding, scaled by sqrt(640) at the input). Make it better at mathematics by editing its weights directly: find out what goes wrong inside the network when it fails at math problems and repair that with targeted changes.

EVALUATION. After you finish, your edited model and the unedited model are evaluated on MATH-500, which you never see, with this fixed protocol: the model's chat template; the user message is the problem, a newline and "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}."; sampling with temperature 1.0, top_k 64, top_p 0.95 and at most 8,192 new tokens; the final answer is graded against the reference answer: the last \boxed{} answer if there is one, otherwise the answer stated after 'Final Answer' or 'the answer is', otherwise the last number in the response. Only weight values can change (same tensor names and shapes).

SCOPE. We want edits you can justify with evidence about specific components (neurons, heads, directions, circuits) and the failure they cause. Repairing a specific failure mechanism you find is in scope. Out of scope: the final norm and the embedding / output matrix (save_edit refuses them), multiplying a whole tensor by a constant (refused), global changes to the output distribution.

METHOD.
1. Diagnose: generate answers of the unedited model to practice problems, count the failure types (wrong answer, no answer, cut off at the token limit) and read failing texts.
2. Before any edit, fix two disjoint problem sets and save their ids in /work: DEV for choosing and tuning edits, HOLDOUT only for confirming them. Never choose or tune on HOLDOUT. Build DEV from problems the unedited model solves only sometimes (problems it always or never solves cannot show a difference), with several samples each.
3. Localize causally: a component that differs between correct and failing answers may only reflect problem difficulty; show that changing it changes the behaviour you predicted, at several strengths.
4. Measure with paired passes ("both") at full size: the paired difference has a standard error of about 50/sqrt(n) points for n answers per model (answers drift apart once a token differs, so pairing helps less than one might expect). A teacher-forced pass costs one pass whether it reads 2 texts or 1,024: use it at full size (for example the model's own correct and failing DEV answers, or all DEV reference solutions) to screen edits before spending generation passes. Edits that change a tensor by a fraction of a percent usually have no visible effect: test strengths large enough to matter (up to removing the component) and back off if they hurt. Judge edits by accuracy, not by fewer cut-offs or shorter answers.
5. Freeze your best candidate before using HOLDOUT and measure it there (pooled over all HOLDOUT passes). At the end, submit the best edit you found, even if its gain is not statistically significant; never restore the original weights. Report its measured gain with its standard error honestly.
6. Before every pass, write in /work/notes.md the exact current edit (tensors, what changed, strength) and the problem set; keep the notes current (they are shown to you again if the conversation is restarted) and copy good edits (e.g. /work/edited to /work/best_1/).
7. Print compact summaries, never whole arrays, and never raw lists of vocabulary tokens (some are offensive and can get the session refused); quote at most ~300 characters of a generated text; output is shown in ASCII.
