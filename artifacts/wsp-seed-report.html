Seed Ideas After Weight-Space Programming

A skeptical review of "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming" (WSP): where the evidence is thin, what the prior art already covers, and six seed ideas with a metric, baseline, novel delta, cheap kill test and kill policy. Three of the six are controls, not bets.

§0Verdict

WSP shows that a frontier LLM plus a harness can produce working weights. It does not show that the weight medium is better than code, steering, or a trained encoder. Most of its headline gains have cheap alternative explanations that nobody has ruled out.

The best next work is therefore a short set of controls (Seeds C, A, D). Only after they pass is it worth funding the real bets (Seeds B, E, F).

§1Where WSP is weak

Ordered by how much each point threatens the claims. Numbers come from the post and from the logs repo.

1.1 Experiment 8: one run per budget, three different edits

  • The post reports one run for each of 10, 25 and 50 passes. There is no variance estimate.
  • MATH-500 rises with every budget: 7.9, 12.3, 14.1, 48.1. GSM8K (25.7 → 25.1) and IFEval (49.0 → 39.9) dip at 25 passes.
  • Two of the three edits target the same loop direction at different scope: a rank-one removal in 30 matrices (10 passes) and 64 columns in the MLPs of layers 15 and 19 (50 passes). Only the 25-pass run differs: it zeroes one attention head.
  • Unpublished rows in eval/RESULTS_excerpt.md show high run-to-run variance. A 5-pass run and a shorter-prompt 10-pass run submitted no edit (7.9). A second 25-pass prompt reproduced 14.1 and 25.1 exactly.
  • The 50-pass run took 158 turns and 20.3 hours. The hours include a 4.7-hour outage.
Corrected claim. "More passes help" is untested. What is shown is that the narrower loop-direction edit at 50 passes worked once.

1.2 Most of the gain is loop removal, and cheap fixes are not ruled out

  • The authors say so themselves: the gains came from removing loops. The share of MATH-500 answers cut at the token limit falls from 93% to 33%.
  • The evaluation already applies presence penalty 1.5, so a plain decoding fix is partly in the baseline.
  • The 7.9 baseline may be an artifact of the thinking-mode evaluation. 93% truncation at a 32,768-token cap suggests the model barely finishes. Compare against thinking off and the published score before crediting any edit.
  • Open question: do cheap non-LLM fixes match 48.1? Candidates are thinking off, Qwen's recommended sampling, a stronger repetition penalty, and a scripted direction ablation (REFUSAL-DIR, SEAL, SOPHIA-LOOP).

1.3 Experiment 5 mixes "write weights" with "design and hand-compile a program"

  • The prompt contains the game's exact source file. The model has to design the policy from that source and hand-encode it, with no tools, one reply, and no feedback.
  • TRACR automates the compiling step for a given program. It does not do the policy design, so Experiment 5 is not simply "what Tracr does". It is closer to designing a program and compiling it by hand.
  • The missing baseline is an LLM-written code policy (PROG-POLICY-ATARI, LLM-GS).
  • The PPO baselines are a 32-unit network at 100M and 500M steps, and the post shows the best seed. Each game is one reply (n=1).
  • Seaquest and Asterix: PPO at 500M steps beats the written network (88.9 vs 53.1, 55.2 vs 53.2).

1.4 Experiment 6: the practice opponent is a near-copy of the test opponents

  • The "strong" practice opponent is a PPO network trained with the same recipe as the hidden evaluators (seed 1, versus seeds 0 and 2). Passes therefore amount to hill-climbing on a near-copy of the test set.
  • It is one run per budget. The same-size PPO baseline had a three-value learning-rate scan. Untuned PPO lost 92 to 98% of games.
  • Fix for any follow-up: require held-out opponents from another family.

1.5 Experiment 7: the attention tool did not "solve" it

  • Weights alone failed 4 of 4 attempts. With the attention tool it picked the sycophant 3 of 3. That happens by chance 1 time in 8, and pair 3 was answered at 55% confidence.
  • The authors' own patching check on pair 1 failed. Last line of runs/attention_tool/ask_devemb_s2.log: "gap closed by astra's 10 components 0.00 (largest change 0.00, random mean 0.00) | MARGIN closed: astra 0.04, largest change 0.08, random mean 0.19". Astra's named components closed less margin than random ones.
  • In weights-only attempt 3 the named components also closed 0.0 of the gap.
  • The attention pattern it used (heads that attend to the user's suggestion) is already documented by SYC-HEADS.

1.6 The reading experiments have no statistical power

  • Experiment 1 has 4 networks, Experiment 2 has 3, and Experiment 3 has 3 networks with 2 conversations each.
  • Experiment 1 used one network per game, chosen from three trained seeds. This is selection on top of n=4.
  • There is no trivial-feature baseline (for example per-channel first-layer energy). There is no comparison with learned weight-space encoders (MODELZOO-PT, WSL-HF).

1.7 Other limits

  • Experiment 4 (fix the car) is a single one-shot edit.
  • One closed model at maximum effort, one lab, no replication with open models. The cost of the loop is never compared with the benefit.
  • No scale story. Weights go in as text up to about 170k values. Larger networks work only through a code sandbox, which is "LLM plus code".
  • Edits do not add ability. Gemma 3 270M, which rarely loops, did not improve: MATH-500 went from 6.8 to between 7.0 and 7.7. The method may only remove a specific obstacle.

T2L, DND, ZHYPER and ORAL generate weights, but through trained hypernetworks or diffusion. None has a general LLM write weights by reasoning. That is the part of WSP that is new.

§3Research purpose

Purpose. Find out whether the weight-space medium itself is useful. WSP shows that a frontier LLM with a harness can produce weights. It does not show that weights are a better edit target than code, steering, or a trained encoder.

Research questions

  1. RQ1. Does writing weights beat writing code or steering, at an equal LLM budget?
  2. RQ2. Does an LLM-written network save environment steps when used as an initialization?
  3. RQ3. Can an LLM read weights better than trivial statistics or learned weight-space encoders, at n ≥ 200?
  4. RQ4. Does an LLM auditor beat a scripted recipe once the audit must produce a causal edit?

Why this matters

WSP is cited as an early result for auditing, initialization, steering and self-improvement. If the controls erase the gains, the field avoids a hype cycle. If a regime survives, it becomes the targeted claim that deserves a full paper.

§4Seed ideas

Seed C · Scripted loop surgery CONTROL

closest_prior_work
REFUSAL-DIR, SEAL, SOPHIA-LOOP, CIRCULAR, STEERVEC, SMALL-LOOP, SPARKS-WSP
novel_delta
A scripted pipeline compared with the WSP Experiment 8 edit under the same rules: collect looping and non-looping generations, take the mean activation difference, orthogonalize the writing matrices, and sweep layers. The new part is a fair, repeated comparison of LLM-agent discovery against a scripted recipe, plus transfer to other looping small models.
Why needed
It tells us whether the LLM contributes discovery or only runs a known recipe.
Metric
MATH-500, GSM8K, IFEval, cut share, number of weights changed. Report the script / LLM gain ratio with a confidence interval.
Baseline
The released 50-pass edit (the repo has apply_edit.py), thinking off, Qwen's recommended sampling, a stronger repetition penalty, SEAL-style steering.
cheap_kill_test
One model, one day. Run the script and its sweep under the LLM's rules: selection on practice problems only, at most 50 forward passes of 512 problems. Include column-sparse and per-layer variants. Otherwise the comparison is rigged, because the 10-pass rank-one edit reached only 12.3. Compare with the 50-pass edit and at least 3 new LLM runs at 50 passes.
Read the result
This is a control, not a seed with a kill, because both outcomes inform. A script at 80% or more of the 50-pass gain undercuts WSP (about MATH-500 40 or above). A script well below it supports WSP. If the script passes, the seed is incremental over REFUSAL-DIR. Do not fund it unless it transfers to at least 3 models.
Obstacle
Probably done in spirit by REFUSAL-DIR plus SEAL. The value is the control.

Seed A · Weights vs code vs code compiled to weights CONTROL

closest_prior_work
SPARKS-WSP, PROG-POLICY-ATARI, LLM-GS, TRACR, EVOLLM
novel_delta
A matched-budget comparison with the same LLM, game, token cap and rollouts across four arms: LLM-written weights, LLM-written Python policy, LLM-written code compiled or distilled into the 32-unit network, and PPO. No prior work isolates the medium.
Why needed
Without it, "LLMs write weights" cannot be separated from "LLMs design game logic".
Metric
Mean MinAtar return over 1,000 episodes (sticky 0.1, 10,000-step cap) at a fixed LLM token budget.
Baseline
WSP Experiment 5 (Breakout 517.4, Seaquest 53.1, Asterix 53.2), PPO at 100M and 500M steps, the LLM-code policy.
cheap_kill_test
Breakout and Asterix. Use the strongest model available, not only open-weight models, because a weak model failing at both arms says nothing. Run 5 zero-feedback replies per game to match Experiment 5.
Read the result
WSP never claimed weights beat code. If code is at least as good as weights at matched tokens, weights have no advantage as an edit target, and the existence claim still stands. I expect the code arm to win.
Obstacle
PROG-POLICY-ATARI probably already shows code policies are competitive with RL.

Seed D · A powered reading benchmark CONTROL

closest_prior_work
SPARKS-WSP, MODELZOO-PT, WSL-HF, DIT, MAIA
novel_delta
At least 200 networks per task with known ground truth, held-out families, and calibrated probabilities. It compares the LLM with statistics baselines, a learned weight-space encoder, and DIT-style adapters. The original had n of 3 or 4.
Metric
Accuracy and Brier score on held-out families.
Baseline
A WSL-HF-style backbone, and statistics baselines at matched supervision: nearest-centroid with 0 to 4 labeled networks, or the same labeled networks given to the LLM in context. A logistic regression trained on 100 labels is not a fair baseline for a zero-shot LLM.
cheap_kill_test
100 small policy networks for 4 games. If the matched-supervision statistics baseline equals the LLM, the reading claim reduces to feature engineering.
Obstacle
MODELZOO-PT and WSL-HF supply the learned baselines and may already cover much of this.

Seed B · LLM-written initialization, then gradient fine-tuning BET

closest_prior_work
SPARKS-WSP, DND, PROG-POLICY-ATARI
novel_delta
The authors list "cheaper optimization and initialization" as an implication but do not test it. This seed measures environment steps to a target score from an LLM-written network that PPO then fine-tunes. DND initializes LoRA through a trained generator. This uses an untrained general LLM.
Why needed
It is the only extension with a plain economic payoff.
Metric
Environment steps to reach a target score, and area under the learning curve.
Baseline
Random-init PPO, and a behavior-cloned network distilled from an LLM code policy.
cheap_kill_test
Run it on Seaquest, not Breakout. On Breakout the written network (517.4) already beats 500M-step PPO (25.3), so "steps to match PPO-500M" is zero. On Seaquest the written network (53.1) is below PPO-500M (88.9). Alternatively set the target to 1.5 times the better of the two. Give PPO 20% of the usual steps. Kill if the saving is under 2x, or if early PPO updates destroy the written structure (return below random-init in the first 5% of steps).
Obstacle
Hand-built networks may be brittle under entropy bonuses. This is empirical.

Seed E · Causal-patch auditing BET

closest_prior_work
AUDIT-AGENTS, HIDDEN-OBJ, TRIGGER-HAYSTACK, DIT, SYC-HEADS, ACTORACLE, MAIA
novel_delta
The auditor must output a localized weight edit that beats the WSP-style random and largest-change patch baselines. WSP already scored its named components by patching them from one model into the other, so "scored on behavior change" alone is not new.
Metric
Gap closed by the edit against random and largest-change components, edit specificity (target behavior change against capability KL), and detection rate at a fixed false-positive rate on at least 30 organisms.
Baseline
AUDIT-AGENTS (13% single, 42% aggregated), the SYC-HEADS scripted recipe, ACTORACLE, DIT.
cheap_kill_test
Do not use the plain sycophancy organisms for detection. With a forward pass, they are found in one query: the sycophant follows 100% of wrong guesses, and the base model already follows every wrong guess. Use organisms whose behavior is hidden or triggered (backdoors, as in TRIGGER-HAYSTACK), or block behavioral queries. Kill if the LLM edit does not beat the random and largest-change patches, or does not beat the scripted SYC-HEADS recipe.
Obstacle
SYC-HEADS and SYC-LIE-CIRCUIT already localize this behavior with scripts.

Seed F · Edit through SPD components BET, HIGH RISK

closest_prior_work
SPD, SPD-TF, ROME, MEMIT, LOC-EDIT, SPARKS-WSP
novel_delta
Give the LLM sparse rank-1 mechanism components as its edit vocabulary, instead of raw matrices.
Metric
Behavior removal against collateral damage on a toy network with known mechanisms.
Baseline
The same LLM editing raw weights.
cheap_kill_test
A toy MLP or tiny transformer with planted mechanisms. Kill if component-level editing does not beat raw editing on specificity.
Obstacle
SPD has not been shown at 0.8B scale. LOC-EDIT warns that localization does not guarantee edit success. Do not fund it beyond the toy test.
Portfolio note. Seeds A, C and D are all controls of the same kind, so the list is less orthogonal than the brief asked for. The real bets are B (on Seaquest), E (with the new delta) and F (toy scale). Run the controls first, because they decide whether the bets are worth funding.

§5Metric and kill policy

  • Pre-register every success threshold before running. No post-hoc metric changes.
  • Same evaluation harness as WSP where possible, so results compare directly.
  • Budget cap per seed: 2 GPU-days, and a cap on LLM tokens and dollars. API cost dominates here. The 50-pass WSP run took 158 turns over 20 hours at maximum effort.
  • One failed kill test ends a bet. A control reports its result either way.
  • At least 5 seeds wherever a result claims a gain. The original's n=1 is the failure to avoid.
  • Held-out opponents or benchmarks from a different family than any practice data.

§6Recommended order

  1. Seed C first (about one day). It confirms or undercuts the headline Qwen claim. I expect the script to land near the LLM result, but run it.
  2. Seed A, then Seed D (a few days each). They decide whether anything is left to build on.
  3. Seed B only if Seed A shows the weight medium holds up. It is the most winnable bet.
  4. Seed E if auditing is the goal. Seed F only as a toy-scale bet.

Seeds A, C and D may produce negative or incremental results. That is better known now than presented later as a discovery.

§8Caveats

Search depth. The prior-art search used standard-mode web search and returned abstracts and snippets. I did not read full papers. Several 2026 arXiv IDs come from snippets and are unchecked. A paper that I did not retrieve could change a "novel_delta" judgment.
Verification. I checked the Experiment 7 patching line in the logs repo myself. The other review corrections come from the reviewer's reading of the READMEs and results tables. I did not re-open each one.
Source status. WSP is a blog post plus a logs repo, not a peer-reviewed paper. I read the repo's READMEs, harness folder and code headers. I did not open all 8,097 files.
